Robert Mapplethorp was a photographer working in New York in the 1980s and early 90s, Mapplethorp became notorious for his photographs of gay men, often naked, sexually unambiguous such as Man in a Polyester Suit which shows an African-american man in the eponymous suit resting his penis on a stool, and another photograph showing a man urinating into another’s mouth, both men dressed in leather. Photographs like these led to him being attacked 
by the Christian right and his exhibitions denounced as pornography. The fact that museums spent public money on exhibiting Mapplethorp's work led to a full scale row in the United States senate and other artists were dragged into
the debate. Senator Jesse Helms led the attack supported
 by The Association for the American Family and religious organisations. The result was a substantial decrease in the amount of support given to artist by the National Endowment for the Arts, the United States government arts subsidy. As well as a questioning of the definition of obscenity laws and a revision of the censorship laws making it easier to close an exhibition or require special conditions to be met. There were many counter claims of discrimination on the grounds of sexuality. Mapplethorp said of his work that he photographed images of love and trust, and that even the most ‘sado masochistic’ of his images represented supreme trust, love and vulnerability on the part of the participants. His defenders claimed that if his images were of heterosexual couples there would have been no such outcry.
