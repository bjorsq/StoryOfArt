The Story of Art

Emma Kay, made with e-2

Emma Kay’s work reconstructs epic histories, geographies and fictions entirely from memory. The Story of Art is her history of art, from the first marks made by humans to the end of the twentieth century, written from memory in 108,812 words.

It is an animated wall projection. Sentences, wrapped into short justified paragraphs, travel towards the viewer from a point just above the centre of the screen, rest there briefly, then accelerate past, off the bottom of the screen; each section heading leaves by the top. The piece speeds up imperceptibly over 9 and a half hours, from a new paragraph every 10s to one every 0.60s, until only the occasional word can be read.

It was projected 25 feet wide at Tate Modern, London, where it looped continuously for 11 months, and then shown at the Power Plant, Toronto. e-2 made it in SVG, so that the artist’s text could be edited up to the last minute and the whole piece regenerated in a single step, and so that the text stayed sharp at that scale.

The original ran in Adobe’s SVG Viewer, a browser plugin which no longer exists. In 2026 it was redeveloped from the original files to run in a modern browser, drawing the letters with the same embedded font, and rendered to video.
